# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

FinancialFFT.jl is a Julia package that prices European options (vanilla, binary, and smile variants) via Fourier/characteristic-function methods. It is an extension of [FinancialMonteCarlo.jl](https://github.com/rcalxrc08/FinancialMonteCarlo.jl): it reuses that package's processes (`BlackScholesProcess`, `MertonProcess`, `KouProcess`, NIG, VarianceGamma, …), `EuropeanOption`, `ZeroRate`, `Underlying`, and adds new `pricer` methods, so the only export is `pricer` plus the method configurator structs.

## Architecture

Entry point [src/FinancialFFT.jl](src/FinancialFFT.jl) includes, in order: `abstracts`, `mods` (every scalar `_mod` hook), `extrema_computation_opt` (the Chernoff range, `compute_chernorff_limits`), `utils` (shared utilities: grids and types, FFT, Clenshaw, payoff conversions), `charexp`, then the methods (`carrmadan`, `carrmadan_lewis`, `fft_parameters`, `lewis`, `cos`), and the densities: `density` (the interface only), `utils_density` (shared by the densities and their pricers), `cos_density`, `fft_density`.

**Densities** ([src/methods/density.jl](src/methods/density.jl), the interface only): `density(process, zero_rate, method, T)` is the interface, like `pricer`, for callers that need a density without a closed formula (Vibrato, likelihood ratio method): it returns an `AbstractDensity` of the log-return `log(S_T / S_0)`, independent of `S_0`, zero outside `[a, b]`. The interface defines `f(y)` (a number, on the CPU) and `density_values(f, y)` (an array) for every density, from two functions a density type implements without the mask: `density_value(f, y)` at a number and `density_array(f, y[, mode])` at an array (default: `density_value` at each point; the mode lets a pricer pick the CPU kernel). `mask_outside_range` (in `utils_density.jl`) zeroes them outside the range. A method that computes a density subtypes `AbstractDensityMethod` and adds a `density` method; with an `M` field it also prices through the one `pricer` of `utils_density.jl`, `density_integral_price`: Simpson's rule for the put on a log-moneyness grid ending at the strike (constant for differentiation: `ignore_derivatives_mod`), call by parity; a smile shares the density, so its prices are the ones of the single options. `CosDensityMethod(N, M)` ([src/methods/cos_density.jl](src/methods/cos_density.jl)) gives a `CosDensity` on the range of COS: `density_value` is a Clenshaw recurrence (`clenshaw_cos_sum`), `density_array` broadcasts and a reduction only, with the coefficients as broadcast arguments (GPU), and in `SerialMode` the recurrence per node (`map`), which `density_values` of a CPU density at an `Array` uses too (`CosDensity{<:Array}`: 7-10 times faster than the N×K broadcast at 1000 points, no allocation but the result). `FFTDensityInverter(N, M)` ([src/methods/fft_density.jl](src/methods/fft_density.jl); `DensityInverter`, which priced with a density in log-moneyness and needed `xmax`, was removed in its favour) gives an `FFTDensity`: one FFT (`fft_density_values`) on the Chernoff range of COS, and cubic Lagrange interpolation on four nodes (`lagrange_cubic`), with gathers, so that every back-end differentiates it and it runs on the device; the range goes through `ignore_derivatives_mod`, a zero-derivative primitive in the Mooncake extension (Mooncake ignores `@ignore_derivatives`, and a grid moving with the parameters adds the derivative of the interpolation error to the scores). The FFT input is Hermitian (#44): the exponent is evaluated on the frequencies `k = 0, …, 2^(N-1)` only, on a constant grid `u_im = -i u_k` (the minus sign, since `brfft` has the `+i` sign), with the phase `(drift - corr - a) u_im` (anchoring the grid at `a` replaces the signs `(-1)^k`), and transformed by `brfft_mod` ([src/methods/utils.jl](src/methods/utils.jl)): FFTW's complex-to-real `brfft` (CUFFT on the device, FFTA for other floats), with methods in every AD extension (components for DualNumbers, HyperDualNumbers, TaylorSeries; one batched `brfft` for ForwardDiff; a tape instruction for ReverseDiff; a primitive for Mooncake; a ChainRules `rrule` for Zygote, since the rule of AbstractFFTs builds its weights on the CPU). The adjoint is `brfft_adjoint`, `rfft` with weights 1, 2, …, 2, 1 (a PatternVector; an `rfft` plan can be passed). Planning a real FFTW transform costs 38-114 µs (2^10-2^18), as much as a transform of 2^14: the extensions that transform several components (DualNumbers, HyperDualNumbers, TaylorSeries) share one `plan_brfft` (FFTW's or FFTA's; TaylorSeries writes into one output buffer with `mul_mod!`, FFTW's `mul!` or a copy for FFTA, which has no `mul!` for real transforms, and reuses one input buffer), the forward rules of Mooncake one plan for primal and tangent, and ReverseDiff keeps its plans (`fft`/`bfft`, `brfft`/`rfft`) in the cache of the tape instruction, reused by a compiled tape; a single transform per call (Zygote, Mooncake's reverse rules, ForwardDiff's batch) has nothing to reuse. The ReverseDiff `brfft_mod` returns its `TrackedArray`, not a vector of tracked reals: the broadcasts after it are one instruction each (ReverseDiff 52-64% faster). The Nyquist node enters through its real part. Negative values (ripples of slow-decaying cases, VG at short maturities) are floored at `eps`, no longer reflected by `abs`. Measured on Merton against the full transform (N = 14, 18): density and price 40-47% faster, every CPU back-end 25-64% (Taylor1 the least, ReverseDiff the most), memory 43% less; on the GPU 17-32% at N = 18, noise at N ≤ 14 (kernel launches dominate). A transform written as a half-length complex FFT (`fft` plus packing, which all `fft` overloads would have covered) was rejected: its preprocessing made it twice as slow as `brfft`. #43 can reuse `brfft_mod` for the Carr-Madan methods. It replaces the FFT density of VibratoMonteCarlo.jl (`lrm_aug.jl`), to be removed there after the next release (VibratoMonteCarlo issue #37 says how). The untracked `density_cos.jl` is the original sketch. Order matters (later files use earlier definitions).

**Quadratures and accuracy floors** (#88): every quadrature has a comment naming the rule and its order: Simpson in Carr-Madan and Carr-Madan-Lewis (an even number of nodes, the last panel not closed), Simpson on `M` intervals in the density pricers (`density_integral_price`), the rectangle rule of Lewis, the trapezoidal rule behind the FFT density, the trapezoidal rule in `log v` of `fft_parameters.jl`; COS has none. The floors of the convergence figure are not from the Fourier quadrature: Simpson with the default `M = 2^10` limits the density pricers to ~5e-10 (O(M⁻⁴)), and the fixed log-strike step `2π / A` the Carr-Madan methods (strike interpolation). Keep a comment on any new quadrature.

**Dispatch design.** Each method is a configurator struct subtyping `FinancialMonteCarlo.AbstractMethod` via `AbstractFFTMethod` (Carr-Madan, Carr-Madan-Lewis; use `fft`) or `AbstractIntegralMethod` (Lewis, COS; direct quadrature/cosine sums), `AbstractDensityMethod <: AbstractIntegralMethod` for the density methods. Pricing is invoked as `pricer(process, zero_rate, method, payoff)`, extending `FinancialMonteCarlo.pricer`, always with four arguments: the execution mode is a field of the method (FinancialMonteCarlo 0.6, #55), `mode` as a keyword of every public constructor (default `SerialMode()`), and the inner constructors take every field positionally, so that `FinancialMonteCarlo.with_mode(method, mode)` (`Accessors.@set`) rebuilds the method. The pricers and `density` read it with `FinancialMonteCarlo.execution_mode(method)` and move the payoff to the device of the mode with `FinancialMonteCarlo.to_mode` (a smile with host strikes runs in `CudaMode`, one with device strikes in `SerialMode`); the internal kernels keep a `mode` argument. The five-argument `pricer` and `density` (trailing mode) are deprecated for one release (end of `density.jl`). Every method has one `pricer` for options and smiles (`FourierPayoff`): several strikes are priced through smiles, options with different maturities by broadcasting `pricer.`, and there is no `pricer` taking a vector of strikes or an array of options.

**Characteristic exponents** ([src/methods/charexp.jl](src/methods/charexp.jl)): per-process `characteristic_exponent_i(imv, process)` (scalar) and `characteristic_exponent_vi` (broadcast), implemented as real functions of an imaginary-argument input (`ψ(v) = ψ_i(v*im)`) to avoid complex arithmetic. A new process needs these methods. A broadcast of `characteristic_exponent_i` over a lazy argument (`v .* im .+ c`) goes to `characteristic_exponent_vi` through a `Base.broadcasted` overload, in a loop of its own: faster than fusing the scalar exponent into the surrounding broadcast (Lewis 0.68 against 0.95 ms, FFT density 1.60 against 1.93 ms on Merton without it), at the cost of one array; an array argument is broadcast element by element. Its ChainRules `rrule` (Zygote) takes the value and the pullback of `characteristic_exponent_vi` from one `rrule_via_ad` in the forward pass (#77; recomputing it in the pullback cost 5-25% of a gradient, CPU and GPU, Float64 and Float32). Methods call the three-argument `characteristic_exponent_i(imv, process, T)`, the log of `E[exp(imv X_T)]`, which defaults to `ψ_i * T`: a process that is not Lévy (Heston, nonlinear in `T`) defines the three-argument `_i`/`_vi` instead. `charexp_and_ders.jl` (untracked) is WIP for derivatives.

**Carr-Madan** ([src/methods/carrmadan.jl](src/methods/carrmadan.jl)): damped call transform, without intrinsic value: the undamped time value has a kink at the forward and decays like `1/v²`. The damping `α` is capped by `exponential_moment_bound`, and `A / 2^Npow` must be small with respect to it. The binary call is minus the strike derivative of the call: `α + iv` leaves the denominator, and the price is divided by `K`. Both Carr-Madan methods interpolate the FFT output at the strikes with `grid_interpolate` ([src/methods/utils.jl](src/methods/utils.jl)): 6-point Lagrange (`lagrange_quintic`, order `h⁶`, about 65 times more accurate than the cubic spline it replaced, issue #39) with gathers, whose pullback is a scatter, so every back-end differentiates it, on the device too; a number on the device is interpolated as a one-point array and summed (CUDA extension). The interpolant is only C⁰: strike derivatives jump at the nodes by about 1e-8. Both Carr-Madan kernels transform `w .* exp.(ψ(…) .+ v_im .* c)` (#45): the factors that do not depend on the model (signs `(-1)^j`, Simpson weights, `dx/3`, the inverse of the denominator with one real division, `inv_denominator`) are floats even when the exponent is a dual, the drift is a phase in the exponent, and the real constant of the exponent (`-(1 + α) corr`, `correction / 2`) leaves the FFT for the scalar factor of the prices. Carr-Madan computes the weights in a loop of their own (`carr_madan_weights`, inside `@ignore_derivatives`), which measured faster; Carr-Madan-Lewis computes them in the broadcast, with the nodes `complex(one_half, x)` and a float `one_half`: a `Rational` added to a complex number in a lazy argument of the exponent (`1 // 2 + x * im`) makes it 2.3 times slower (Lewis uses a float `one_half` for the same reason). Keep rationals out of complex lazy arguments. Measured on Merton against the previous commit: Carr-Madan 35% faster (price) and 19% (ForwardDiff), Carr-Madan-Lewis unchanged.

**Carr-Madan parameters from a tolerance** ([src/methods/fft_parameters.jl](src/methods/fft_parameters.jl), issue #41, suite `fft_parameters`): `CarrMadanMethod(ε; α, Npow_max)` and `CarrMadanLewisMethod(ε; Npow_max)` choose `A`, `Npow` at pricing time. Both methods hold a `grid` field, `FFTGrid(A, Npow)` or `FFTTolerance(ε, Npow_max)` (defined in `carrmadan.jl`), and the pricers call `fft_grid(method.grid, method, process, zero_rate, payoff)`, inside `@ignore_derivatives`, with `A_min`, `b_min` through `ignore_derivatives_mod` (ReverseDiff would track the Chernoff bound, Mooncake would differentiate `A`): the parameters are constants on every back-end (tested). Interpolation and truncation: `scale/π ∫ |ψ(v)| w(2πv/A) dv` with `w = min(L₆ θ⁶, 1 + Λ₆)` below `A` and 1 above (L₆, Λ₆ of the six-point stencil at t = 1/2), trapezoid in log v, `≤ ε/2`, bisection for `A_min`; measured it binds the error, not the truncation, which this weight bounds too. Aliasing: Simpson's weights are the trapezoidal ones times `1 - (-1)^j/3`, so besides the Poisson copies at `2b` there are copies at `±b` (half period = half-width of the strike grid) with weight `-1/3`, almost independent of `k` (checked numerically); each side `≤ ε/4`, from closed forms (`G ≤ F0`, `≤ e^{-rT}`, `≤ K e^{-rT}`) or a Chernoff bound (`AliasingExponent`, minimised with `chernoff_optimum`, shared with the COS range). Then `Npow = ceil(log2(A_min b_min / π))`, `A = π 2^Npow / b_min`. Measured errors are 0.25-0.57 ε on six models.

**Payoff handling** ([src/methods/utils.jl](src/methods/utils.jl)): everything but COS and the density pricers is priced as a call and converted with `call_to_put` (dispatching on vanilla/binary/smile option types). COS and the density pricers expand the put and convert it with `put_to_option`: the coefficients of a call grow like `exp(b)`, so a call needs the right tail under the share measure, which the range does not cover and widening it would only trade for rounding errors. The binary call is minus the strike derivative of the Lewis formula: Carr-Madan-Lewis shares one kernel, with `lewis_denominator` and `carr_madan_lewis_call` selecting the payoff. The `fft` overloads of the AD back-ends take vectors: every FFT is of a vector.

**Truncation range of the COS method** ([src/methods/extrema_computation_opt.jl](src/methods/extrema_computation_opt.jl)): `[a, b]` comes from a Chernoff bound on the tails of the log-price, `t(s) = (T ψ_i(mul s) - log ε) / s` minimised over `s` (issue #38): `t'(s)` has the sign of `g(s) - L`, `g = s κ' - κ` (increasing for a convex exponent `κ`), `L = -log ε`, and one complex step of `κ` gives `g` (real part `κ`, imaginary part over `h` `κ'`; `chernoff_g`); `mul = 1` is the upper tail, so the endpoint is `driftT + mul t`, and only skewed models tell the two sides apart. The complex step is `eps(s)`, except for Heston (`sqrt(eps(s))`), whose imaginary parts cancel when `γ` is imaginary. The bracket starts at `s_min = 1` and at the Gaussian guess `s sqrt(L / g(s))` from it (exact for Black Scholes, `~1/√T`, beyond the root for heavier tails), moved to the next guess, at least doubled, while `g < L`, and halved towards the lower end where the exponent overflows (`g` not finite); then Brent's method (`brent_root`) in the coordinates `u = log(s / (B - s))` (`log s` for `B = ∞`; `BoundedCoordinate`/`UnboundedCoordinate`, chosen once by dispatch, not tested at every evaluation) and `log(g - g0)`, where `g` is a line both for a Gaussian and near a pole of the exponent at the bound `B` (on `g` itself Brent ended in bisection: `g ~ exp(s²)` in Merton, the NIG root 4e-6 below `B`), to a relative tolerance `sqrt(eps(FT))` on `s`: `t` is flat at its minimum, so the endpoints match a solve to 1e-14 to about 1e-13, and any `s` gives a valid, wider, bound. 4-26 evaluations of the exponent per range (both tails) on the models of the tests, against 102-115 for the bisection it replaced (range 3-18 times faster). The Heston `exponential_moment_bound` solves `T / τ(s) - 1 = 0` for the explosion time `τ` with the same `brent_root` (0.7 µs against 4.3 µs for the bisection, which had become half the Heston range), stepping back to the finite side. Still open from #38 (point 1, design to be approved): the search runs in the number type of the model (duals, TaylorN), whose derivative components are discarded; the proposal is one generic `value_model` rebuilding a process from the `v_value_mod` of its fields with `ConstructionBase.constructorof`, not a helper per model. The search never evaluates beyond `B (1 - sqrt(eps))`, with `B = exponential_moment_bound(process, mul[, T])`, where `E[exp(mul s X)]` stops being finite (Kou `λ₊`/`λ₋`, VG/NIG from the log/sqrt argument, Heston from the moment explosion time, hence the `T`; `Inf` otherwise): past it `characteristic_exponent_i` returns meaningless finite values. A new process with semi-heavy tails needs an `exponential_moment_bound` method. The range is for `log(S_T / S0)`: on `y = log(S_T / K)` it moves by `log(S0 / K)`, a constant for AD, and a smile uses the union of the ranges of its strikes. Sharing it shares the payoff coefficients and the phases of the characteristic function, so a strike costs only the series, against 3.5-4.5 times as much with a range per strike; a smile that is wide with respect to the standard deviation needs a larger `N`. The series (`cos_phased_series`) folds the strike-independent phases into complex coefficients once and, in `SerialMode`, sums them per strike with a Clenshaw recurrence (`cos_clenshaw`, evaluated with `map` for ReverseDiff): one `sincos` per strike instead of one `cos` per term and strike, 5-9 times faster for smiles of 100-500 strikes, no gain on a single option, where the characteristic exponent dominates. Other modes (GPU) keep the N×K broadcast `cos(u x + phase)`: the recurrence is sequential in `k` and indexes the coefficients element-wise. The search (`chernoff_optimum`) takes the exponent as a callable (`TailExponent(model, T, mul)` here) with `complex_step` and `exponential_moment_bound` methods, so other bounds reuse it.

**Clenshaw recurrence** ([src/methods/utils.jl](src/methods/utils.jl)): `clenshaw_exp_sum` (complex coefficients as real/imaginary arrays, `Val(true)` for the real part only) and `clenshaw_cos_sum` (real coefficients) are shared by COS, Lewis smiles and `CosDensity` (its `density_value`, and its `density_array` in `SerialMode`). Near θ = 0 or π (`|cos θ| > 0.95`) they switch to Reinsch's form, on `d_k = b_k - σ b_{k+1}`: about 1.8 times slower per term (longer dependency chain), but on the Lewis coefficients, where `θ = h c` is close to 0 at the money, the plain recurrence errs by up to 1e-7 and Reinsch's by 1e-13. Lewis evaluates the exponent on the nodes `v >= 0` only (the integrand is even: `ψ(1/2 - iv) = conj ψ(1/2 + iv)`), with weight 2, 1 for the node 0 of an odd `N` (`LewisMethodResult.weights`), and uses the recurrence for smiles only (`lw_integrate` with the mode; a single option keeps the broadcast, as cheap; outside `SerialMode`, on the GPU, a smile is `exp(c_k / 2) Σ_j a_j cos(v_j c_k + b_j)` through `phased_cos_sum`, the kernel of COS: one `cos` per term and strike (one strike per column through a lazy `transpose(x)`, not a `reshape`, the idiom of FinancialMonteCarlo's smiles too; `density_integral_price` builds its nodes × strikes matrix the same way), everything real, `lewis_amplitudes_phases`, #46), with the nodes `v_j = v_0 + j h` and coefficients from `lewis_coefficients`; smiles return vectors in every mode. Both have ChainRules `rrule`s (#47): Zygote differentiated the element reads of their loops with a dense cotangent per read, O(N²) per call (26 s and 26 GiB for a COS smile of 101 strikes); the rules make one pass over `k`, with `cos kθ`, `sin kθ` by rotation refreshed every 32 terms, giving the coefficient cotangents `(ā + i b̄) e^{-ikθ}` and θ̄ from the plain sums of `S' = Σ i k c_k e^{ikθ}` (no second recurrence). Only Zygote uses them.

**Execution modes / optional backends.** `FinancialMonteCarlo.AbstractMode` (`SerialMode`, `MultiThreading`, `AbstractCudaMode`; `BaseMode` before 0.6) is the `mode` field of every method. Optional packages are package extensions (`[weakdeps]`/`[extensions]` in `Project.toml`, code in [ext/](ext/)): CUDA (`grid_interpolate` of a number on the device; the grids go to the device through `adapt_array`, which is `FinancialMonteCarlo.to_mode`, whose CUDA extension makes `CuArray`s keeping the element type), DualNumbers, HyperDualNumbers, ForwardDiff, TaylorSeries (these supply `fft` overloads and `real_mod`/`imag_mod`/`v_value_mod` hooks so AD works through FFT and the range computation; TaylorSeries covers `Taylor1` and `TaylorN`, for which there is no `cis` (JuliaDiff/TaylorSeries.jl#445): the kernels fold phases into a complex `exp` instead; DualNumbers and HyperDualNumbers have no `sincos`, hence `sincos_mod`, overloaded with one `sincos` of the value in their extensions; and whose element type says neither the order nor the `JetSpace`, so build constants from a value, with `zero(x)`, not from a type; the zero of a common type comes from `zero_promoted(x...)`, `zero(promote_type(...))` of the types, which the extension builds from the values for series), Mooncake (0.5; `fft` declared as a primitive with `frule!!`/`rrule!!`, since Mooncake cannot differentiate the FFTW plan). Mooncake also fails on type-unstable code with small `Union` return types (e.g. `Union{Float16, Float64}`), so keep pricing paths type stable, and it cannot differentiate the complex `sqrt` of Base (it bitcasts): use the `sqrt_mod` hook, overlaid in the extension. On the GPU, Zygote cannot differentiate broadcasts whose intermediate results are tuples (pass real and imaginary parts as separate arrays), ReverseDiff cannot store tracked reals in GPU arrays, Mooncake's CUDA broadcast rules reject arrays mixing real and complex element types (every kernel but the binary Lewis integrand), `mapreduce` other than `+` (the `extrema` of the strikes of a COS smile, inside `@ignore_derivatives`, which Mooncake does not see) and a differentiable `Ref` argument (the FFT density): reported upstream as chalk-lab/Mooncake.jl#1450, tracked in #63, and recorded in the `gpu` suite as `mooncake_gpu` in `cases.jl` (a tolerance for the binary Lewis option, `Broken` elsewhere), and kernels must not index arrays element-wise (`CUDA.allowscalar(false)`; the rrule of `sum` of PatternVectors gives any vector a host `PatternVector` cotangent, and ChainRules then computes `dot(Ȳ, B)` with a device `B`: PatternVectors ≥ 0.1.6 does that `dot` with broadcasts (0.1.7 without ambiguities with ReverseDiff)): the ForwardDiff `fft!` uses only broadcasts and one batched `fft!` with the components along a new last dimension (nested duals of a Hessian recurse into it, one dimension per level). The core code calls hooks like `real_mod`, `imag_mod` (all the scalar ones in [src/methods/mods.jl](src/methods/mods.jl)), `adapt_array` instead of `real`/`imag`/arrays directly; use them when editing numeric kernels. `ChainRulesCore.@ignore_derivatives` is used around non-differentiable setup (pattern vectors, asserts) for Zygote support. ReverseDiff (extension: `fft` recorded as one tape instruction with `bfft` pullback, `v_value_mod` for tracked reals) needs ReverseDiff ≥ 1.18 (1.17 overflows the stack materializing complex broadcasts of tracked values) (compat `1.18`), and PatternVectors ≥ 0.1.4, whose ReverseDiff extension resolves the broadcast style conflict with `TrackedStyle` (the compat is 0.1.7, for `dot` on the GPU). ReverseDiff and Mooncake ignore `@ignore_derivatives`: wrap quantities that must stay constants (truncation ranges, grid bounds) in `ignore_derivatives_mod` inside the macro (`@ignore_derivatives ignore_derivatives_mod.(…)`), or they become tracked and break integer conversions. The hook is `v_value_mod` (dual numbers, Taylor series, tracked reals), a zero-derivative primitive in the Mooncake extension; the macro is still needed for Zygote, and keeps it from tracing the computation. ReverseDiff cannot broadcast a function that captures tracked values: pass them as broadcast arguments, or use `map`.

**Floating-point types** ([src/methods/utils.jl](src/methods/utils.jl), suite `types`): prices keep the float type of the inputs. `float_type(x...)` (the promoted type of the `v_value_mod` of the inputs, `float`ed: no arithmetic on the values) is the type of grids and ranges, and `to_float_type(FT, x)` converts method parameters (`A`, `α`) to it; avoid Float64 literals and `pi * N` (an irrational times an integer is a Float64) in setup code; use the constants of IrrationalConstants (`twoπ`, `invπ`, imported in `src/FinancialFFT.jl`), which take the type of the other operand, and rationals for other constants (`fft_parameters.jl`), converted to the float type once outside loops. The Chernoff range uses `scaled_tolerance(FT, 1e-14)` and a tolerance `sqrt(eps(FT))` on the optimal `s` (also for the aliasing bound of `fft_parameters.jl`). FFTW handles Float32/Float64 only: other floats (BigFloat, DoubleFloats) are transformed by FFTA (#50; a dependency, loaded with `import FFTA`), through the same AbstractFFTs functions and plans (`fft`, `brfft`, `rfft`, `plan_brfft`), on any length; FFTW's methods are more specific, so Float32/Float64 stay on FFTW and `CuArray`s on CUFFT. FFTA was chosen over GenericFFT.jl (actively maintained, faster `brfft`, errors of 1-3 eps against 3-27): against the radix-2 FFT it replaced, `fft` 1.3-2.5 times and `brfft` 1.7-3.6 times faster on Float16, Double64, BigFloat (2^10, 2^14). FFTA has no in-place plans: the `fft!` of the DualNumbers, HyperDualNumbers, ForwardDiff and TaylorSeries extensions call `plan_fft_mod!`/`fft_mod!` (utils.jl), FFTW's `plan_fft!` (CUFFT's on the device) for ComplexF32/ComplexF64 and FFTA's out-of-place plan copied into the argument (`InPlaceFFTPlan`) for other floats, so every back-end differentiates the Carr-Madan methods in BigFloat too (suite `types`). AD element types still reach the `fft`/`brfft_mod` overloads of the extensions. Float16 overflows in the FFT density (not supported).
