Automatic differentiation

Prices are generic functions of the inputs of the model, the curve, and the payoff, so they can be differentiated by the following back-ends, loaded with the corresponding package:

MethodDualNumbersHyperDualNumbersTaylorSeriesForwardDiffReverseDiffZygoteMooncake
LewisMethodyesyesyesyesyesyesyes
CosMethodyesyesyesyesyesyesyes
CarrMadanLewisMethodyesyesyesyesyesnoyes
CarrMadanMethodyesyesyesyesyesnoyes
DensityInverteryesyesyesyesyesyesyes

The test suite checks each entry against the closed formulas of Black and Scholes, for European and binary options and their smiles, with all of the inputs S0, K, r, T, σ, d. Gradients can be taken through the Heston model too. TaylorSeries covers both Taylor1 and TaylorN, which gives the mixed derivatives of several inputs at once:

using FinancialFFT, FinancialMonteCarlo, ForwardDiff

price(x) = pricer(BlackScholesProcess(x[3], Underlying(x[1], x[4])), ZeroRate(x[2]), LewisMethod(600.0, 20000), EuropeanOption(1.0, 100.0))
ForwardDiff.gradient(price, [100.0, 0.02, 0.2, 0.01])
4-element Vector{Float64}:
   0.554049403294252
  47.05553456232842
  39.05541959828385
 -55.40494032942517

TaylorN works in the same way; its variables are declared with variables!, or set_variables before TaylorSeries 0.23:

using TaylorSeries

δS, δσ = TaylorSeries.variables!("δS δσ"; order = 2)
pricer(BlackScholesProcess(0.2 + δσ, Underlying(100.0 + δS, 0.01)), ZeroRate(0.02), LewisMethod(600.0, 20000), EuropeanOption(1.0, 100.0))

Known limitations

Zygote with the FFT methods

The cubic spline of CarrMadanMethod and CarrMadanLewisMethod is built by mutating arrays, so it is excluded from the differentiation. Zygote does not raise an error, but the sensitivities that flow through the FFT are lost: the gradient in σ is zero, and those in r, T and d are wrong. Use another back-end with these methods.

  • ReverseDiff.jl requires version 1.18 or later.
  • On the GPU only ForwardDiff.jl and Zygote.jl are supported, see GPU.
